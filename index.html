A correct crossover network sums flat at every parallel-mix position. The pre-7.0 build mixed the all-pass band sum against an unfiltered dry path, producing two ~30 dB notches at the crossover frequencies. Drag the parallel mix and watch.
Transient shaper
Differential-envelope processing with a channel-linked detector, so the stereo image does not move. Both terms are ratios of the fast and slow envelopes, so the controls behave identically on a quiet mix and a loud one.

What these engines are. Wow and flutter are two sine LFOs modulating a delay line; head bump is a peaking filter at 60 Hz; the HF roll-off is a low-pass; hiss, crackle and rumble are seeded noise generators. This is stylised degradation, not a tape-machine model — there is no hysteresis, no bias, no gap loss.

Determinism. Every noise bed is seeded, so the same project and the same seed always render the same file.

Read this before delivering. These exports are channel beds synthesised from your stereo master. Stereo contains no height information, so the height channels carry decorrelated ambience, not recovered content. The ADM BWF is a DirectSpeakers bed — it is not object-based authoring and it is not a certified Dolby Atmos master. The binaural fold-down is fixed, not head-tracked.

Honest limitations
Signal Rot is an experimental mastering laboratory, not a certified measurement tool. The loudness meter follows BS.1770-4 and EBU Tech 3341/3342 and reproduces the standard's tabulated K-weighting coefficients exactly, but it has not been validated against the EBU compliance material and makes no compliance claim. The true-peak meter is a windowed-sinc polyphase interpolator, not the tabulated BS.1770 Annex 2 filter. Immersive exports are synthesised channel beds. Full detail is in docs/LIMITATIONS.md.
